Ash Cave is the largest recess cave in Ohio: a sandstone horseshoe about 700 feet across and 100 feet deep, with a seasonal waterfall that drops from its rim. It sits at the south end of Hocking Hills State Park, and you reach it on a paved, level quarter-mile trail. For the size of the payoff, it's the easiest walk in the Hocking Hills.

That's why it's the trail we suggest for the first morning of a trip, and for any group with small kids, grandparents or tired legs.

## Ash Cave, Hocking Hills State Park, at a glance

- **Where:** State Route 56, near South Bloomingville, about 30 minutes from downtown Logan
- **Trails:** Gorge Trail, a quarter mile, easy and wheelchair accessible. Rim Trail, a quarter mile, moderate
- **Hours:** a half hour before sunrise to a half hour after sunset, all year
- **Cost:** free, parking included
- **At the trailhead:** restrooms, picnic tables and a historical marker

The trail details come from the Ohio Department of Natural Resources. Its [Hocking Hills State Park page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) lists any closures, so look before you leave the cabin.

## Getting there and where to park

This is the southernmost stop in the park, a short drive south of Old Man's Cave and Cedar Falls. The Ash Cave main parking area is on State Route 56, and signs point you to the Ash Cave trail head from there.

Fall weekends are the busy time, so come early. There are picnic tables by the parking area if you'd like to bring lunch.

## The Gorge Trail in

The Ash Cave Gorge Trail is a quarter mile of asphalt through a narrow gorge lined with tall hemlocks. It's level the whole way. Then the gorge opens all at once, and the cave is in front of you.

The numbers don't prepare you for it. The rim curves about 700 feet from end to end and hangs roughly 90 to 100 feet over a floor of sand. Kids run. Adults go quiet. Walk all the way to the back wall and say something in a normal voice. The cave has carried sound so well, for so long, that early settlers held Sunday services here, with a big fallen slab called Pulpit Rock for the preacher.

## The Ash Cave waterfall

The waterfall drops from the middle of the rim into a small pool at the bottom, about 90 feet below. It's fed by a small stream, so it depends on the weather. Come in spring or the day after a storm for the full effect. In late summer it can thin to a drip.

Winter is the surprise. When it stays cold, the falling water freezes into a natural ice sculpture at the base of the cave.

## The Rim Trail back

To make a loop, take the steps beside the cave up to the Rim Trail. It's another quarter mile along the top of the gorge and it's rated moderate. It brings you back to the parking area. The steps are steep, and this half isn't accessible.

At the top of the steps, a left turn puts you on the trail toward [Cedar Falls](/blog/cedar-falls-hocking-hills), about 2.3 miles away, and on to [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave). That's the Grandma Gatewood Trail, 6 miles from end to end.

## The Ash Cave fire tower

Along that same trail, in Hocking State Forest, stands the Ash Cave fire tower. It's an 80-foot tower built in 1934, back when lookouts watched these hills for smoke. Ohio closed its last working fire tower in 1978, and this is one of the few still standing.

When weather allows, you can climb the stairs at your own risk for a view over the treetops in every direction. The cab at the top is closed. You can also reach the Ash Cave lookout tower by car, on State Route 374.

One caution: as of November 2026, the state forest lists the tower as closed. Check the [Ash Cave Fire Tower page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/ash-cave-fire-tower) or the [Hocking State Forest page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-state-forest) for the latest before you go.

## How the cave got its name

Early settlers found the cave floor covered in ashes. According to the state's history of the site, at least 1 pile was said to be 100 feet long, 30 feet wide and 3 feet deep. A writer who visited in 1837 guessed there were still 300 or 400 bushels left.

Nobody knows for certain where the ashes came from. The usual explanation is centuries of campfires. The Shawnee are thought to have used the cave as a resting place on the route between their villages. The cave became part of the park in 1925.

## Where to take Ash Cave photos

The pictures you've seen are almost all taken from one of 3 places:

1. **The mouth of the gorge,** where the trail opens and the whole rim fits in the frame
2. **The back wall,** looking out past the waterfall
3. **The rim,** from the overlook on the Rim Trail

Put a person in the shot. Without someone standing on the sand, there's nothing to show how big the cave is.

## When to go, season by season

- **Spring:** the waterfall is at its fullest, and the gorge is green with wildflowers
- **Summer:** the gorge stays cool, though the waterfall may be thin
- **Fall:** the trees above the gorge turn, and it's the busiest time of year
- **Winter:** the quietest season, with the ice cone when it's cold enough

December has its own tradition. The park's free holiday afternoon in the cave, long known as Christmas in Ash Cave, is listed this year as Holidays in Ash Cave: Saturday, December 12, 2026, from 3:00 to 5:00 pm, with holiday crafts, music, cookies, hot cocoa and a visit from Santa. The [park's event page](https://ohiodnr.gov/home/news-and-events/all-events/parks-wc-events/SE-Ohio/holiday-ashcave-hocking-12-12-26) has the details. It pairs well with one of our [Christmas cabin rentals](/blog/christmas-cabin-rentals).

January brings the park's [Winter Hike](https://ohiodnr.gov/home/news-and-events/all-events/parks-wc-events/SE-Ohio/winterhike-hocking-11627), which finishes here. This season it's Saturday, January 16, 2027: 6 miles from Old Man's Cave, with hot chocolate and donuts from the Lions Club at the cave and a shuttle back to your car.
