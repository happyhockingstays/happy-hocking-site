Looking for cabins for winter? Here's a little secret we wish more people knew: winter might be the most magical season in the Hocking Hills. When the hills go quiet and the snow settles over the trees, the whole region feels a world away, hushed and soft and beautiful. Step outside your cabin on a winter morning with coffee in hand, and that feeling of being one with nature runs even deeper in the stillness. We're a small family-owned business, and winter is the season we most love letting guests in on. Here's how to do it right.

## Why winter cabin rentals are worth it

A lot of people assume you shouldn't visit in winter: if you can't hike, or a few things are closed, why come? We understand the instinct, and we promise it's wrong.

So much of the magic is right at your cabin. Wake up to a snow-dusted view and watch the flakes fall from a warm hot tub. Let the day be exactly as slow as you want it. The Hocking Hills under snow are achingly beautiful, and you get them with almost none of the crowds. Winter here isn't a lesser season. It's a quieter, cozier, more intimate one.

If your group loves to hike, you still can. Plenty of our guests do. They bring the right gear, check the forecast and know their comfort level, because the trails can be slick when it's cold. Do that, and a winter hike to a frozen waterfall is something you'll talk about for years.

## What to look for in cabins for winter

The heart of a winter weekend is the cabin itself, so choose one built to get warm and stay warm. When we help guests pick winter cabin rentals, these are the things we look at first:

- **A hot tub.** Non-negotiable in January. Every one of our cabins has one, and they're open all year
- **A sauna.** There's a special kind of joy in easing into a sauna after a cold day on the trails
- **A fireplace or a big great room.** Somewhere to gather with a warm drink while it snows
- **A kitchen you can settle into.** Winter weekends are for long breakfasts and slow-cooked dinners
- **An easy drive.** Some cabins sit up hilly gravel drives, so check the forecast and message us before a snowy stay

For a sauna, pick one of these:

- [Valhalla Hills](/cabins/valhalla-hills) has a 3-person sauna and an 8-person hot tub, plus a great room with a stone fireplace and room for 29
- [Pickle Hill](/cabins/pickle-hill) has an infrared sauna for a group of 10 and an indoor pickleball barn for a few games before you warm up
- [Lazy Deer Retreat](/cabins/lazy-deer-retreat) has a private sauna in the primary suite and sits 5 minutes from Old Man's Cave

For a fireplace, [The Johnny](/cabins/the-johnny) has an indoor gas fireplace under its log beams. [Dunkle Creek](/cabins/dunkle-creek) has 2 electric fireplaces in a black A-frame in the woods.

### Winter on the trails

A few tips from our guests who hike all winter:

- Wear boots with real grip, and pack traction cleats if there's ice
- Dress in layers and bring gloves and a hat
- Start with an easy trail like Ash Cave, then decide if you want more
- Check trail conditions on [Explore Hocking Hills](https://www.explorehockinghills.com/) before you go
- Head back before dark. Winter days are short, and the hot tub will be waiting
