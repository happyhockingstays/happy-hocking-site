If you want to buy a cabin in Ohio that pays its way as a rental, the Hocking Hills are where most people start looking. The area draws an estimated 4 million visitors a year, and it's about an hour from Columbus. What decides whether a cabin works is the home itself, the rules where it sits and who runs it.

We co-host vacation homes across the Hocking Hills, and plenty of the owners we work with came to us while they were buying. This is what we'd tell a friend before they make an offer. It isn't financial, legal or tax advice, so bring your lender, agent and accountant in early.

## Buy a cabin in Ohio where the guests already are

The Hocking Hills draw an estimated [4 million visitors a year](https://woub.org/2025/11/21/hocking-county-short-term-rental-businesses-more-regulations/), and the trip is easy from Ohio's biggest cities: about an hour from Columbus, 2.5 hours from Cincinnati and 3.5 from Cleveland. Guests come for the caves and waterfalls at Hocking Hills State Park, and they come back in every season.

- **Summer:** private pools, Lake Logan and long evenings at the fire pit
- **Fall:** color on the hills and cool hiking weather
- **Winter:** hot tubs, fireplaces and frozen waterfalls. Our post on [cabins for winter](/blog/cabins-for-winter) shows why guests book the cold months too
- **All year:** family reunions, friends' trips and group getaways

Be clear-eyed about the other side. Visitors and short-term rentals here have both grown fast since 2020, and some owners say occupancy has dropped. A cabin that gives guests a reason to pick it books. A plain one competes on price.

## Cabins for sale in Hocking Hills: where to look

Hocking Hills homes for sale show up on the big real estate sites and with local agents. A search for houses for sale in Hocking Hills, Ohio pulls in a wide area, so it helps to search by town as well:

- **Logan:** the county seat, near Lake Logan and 20 to 30 minutes from the main trails. Our cabins near town include [Pickle Hill](/cabins/pickle-hill) and [The Farm at Nickel Plate](/cabins/the-farm-at-nickel-plate)
- **The roads around Old Man's Cave:** the closest homes to the trails. [Lazy Deer Retreat](/cabins/lazy-deer-retreat) is about 5 minutes away
- **Laurelville and Sugar Grove:** wooded country on the west and north sides of the hills, about 30 to 40 minutes from Old Man's Cave. [The Johnny](/cabins/the-johnny) is in Laurelville
- **McArthur:** the south side, close to Ash Cave. [Dunkle Creek](/cabins/dunkle-creek) is about 15 minutes from it
- **The Lancaster area:** the closest to Columbus, about 40 minutes from Old Man's Cave

Before you fall for a listing, check which county it's in. Mailing addresses don't always follow county lines, and the county decides the lodging tax and the rules you'll follow. Our guide to the [Hocking County lodging tax](/blog/hocking-county-lodging-tax) covers how registration and monthly filing work there.

## What makes a Hocking Hills cabin rent well

Guests search by feature as much as by place. Here's what we see them look for, and what every home we host has in common.

- **A private pool and a hot tub.** Every cabin we host has both. Pools are seasonal: May 15 to October 15 at most of our homes and April 15 to November 30 at Clouds Reach. Hot tubs run all year. Guests search for [cabins with private pools](/hocking-hills-cabins-with-pool) by name.
- **Room for a group.** Reunions and friends' trips need a home that sleeps 10 or more, with enough bathrooms and a big table. See our [cabin rentals for large groups](/hocking-hills-large-group-cabins) for what that looks like.
- **Something to do without leaving.** A game room, a court, a pond or a lake gives a cabin its story. Think of Pickle Hill's regulation pickleball barn or the sports barn at The Farm.
- **An easy arrival.** A driveway guests can handle in winter, clear directions and a smooth check-in.
- **Wi-Fi that works.** Cell service is spotty in parts of the hills, so guests lean on the Wi-Fi.
- **Bright, real photos.** The listing is the first tour, so the photos have to show the home at its best.

## What to check before you make an offer

Add these to your list before you sign anything:

1. **The county and township.** They set the lodging tax and, soon, maybe a permit.
2. **City limits.** The city of Logan has had its own short-term rental permit since 2023.
3. **HOA and deed restrictions.** Some neighborhoods limit or ban short-term rentals. Ask for the documents.
4. **The county's draft rules.** Hocking County has a draft short-term rental ordinance that's covered driveway standards for emergency vehicles, occupancy limits and a local contact. It's still a draft as of November 2026, but buy as if it could pass.
5. **The septic system and the well.** Both have to keep up with a full house, every weekend. Ask your inspector to look at them with the guest count in mind.
6. **Insurance.** Ask an agent about coverage written for short-term rental use before you close.
7. **The furnishing budget.** Beds, linens, a stocked kitchen, outdoor furniture and games add up fast. Plan for them before the first guest.

## The tax side: talk to a CPA first

Taxes are part of the reason a lot of the owners we work with bought. The rules are specific, so here are the basics to bring to a CPA.

- **Short stays are treated differently.** When the average guest stay is 7 days or less, IRS rules don't treat the home as a "rental activity" for the passive loss rules. Whether losses can then offset other income depends on [material participation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925), which is measured in hours, such as 100 hours a year and more than anyone else.
- **Hours matter when someone helps you run it.** If a co-host or manager handles most of the day-to-day work, ask your CPA how that affects the hours test before you plan around it.
- **Personal use counts.** The [IRS treats a rental home as a residence](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc415) if you use it for more than 14 days a year or 10% of the days it's rented, whichever is greater. That limits what you can deduct.

None of this is tax advice. A CPA who works with short-term rentals can tell you how it applies to you, and it's worth that conversation before you buy.
