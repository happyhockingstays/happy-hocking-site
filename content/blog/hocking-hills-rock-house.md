The Hocking Hills Rock House is a tunnel inside a sandstone cliff: a passage about 200 feet long, halfway up a 150-foot rock face, with a row of window-like openings carved by weather. It's the only true cave in Hocking Hills State Park, and a short trail with stone steps takes you up and through it.

It surprises people. Most expect another overhang, and they walk into a room instead.

## Hocking Hills Rock House at a glance

- **Where:** State Route 374, about 15 minutes' drive from Old Man's Cave
- **Trail:** the Gorge Trail (0.6 mile) and Rim Trail (0.2 mile), one way with steps. The park rates the area moderate
- **Hours:** a half hour before sunrise to a half hour after sunset, every day
- **Cost:** free, parking included
- **At the lot:** picnic tables, grills, restrooms and a shelter house you can reserve
- **Time needed:** about an hour

## What makes the Rock House cave different

Most of the famous spots in the park are recess caves. At [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave) and [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave), water wore away a softer layer of sandstone and left a huge overhang open to the sky. Rock House formed another way.

In Hocking Hills State Park, Rock House sits along cracks that run through the cliff. Over time, weather widened those cracks in the softer middle layer of the sandstone until they became a passage. The result is the Rock House cave: about 200 feet long, 20 to 30 feet wide and 20 to 25 feet high, open at both ends, according to the state's geologists.

Along the outside wall, a row of 7 openings, separated by 5 thick columns of stone, lets light in like windows. The Ohio Geological Survey calls Rock House the state's most unusual natural arch and its longest natural tunnel.

## The Rock House trail

The [park's trail map](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/ohiodnr.gov/documents/parks/parkmaps/hockinghillstrailmap.pdf) shows 2 short trails here: the Rock House Gorge Trail at 0.6 mile and the Rock House Rim Trail at 0.2 mile. Both are signed one way and have steps. The [park's website](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) lists the whole area at 1 mile and rates it moderate.

The trail leads from the lot to the foot of the cliff, and steps cut into the rock take you up and into the cave. Walk the passage end to end, look out through the windows and follow the arrows back up to the lot.

A few tips:

- **Wear shoes with grip.** The steps and the cave floor are sandstone, and they stay damp
- **Let your eyes adjust.** It's dim inside, especially at the back of the passage
- **Stay on the marked trail.** The park requires it everywhere, and the cliff edges are steep
- **Bring ice cleats in winter.** The park recommends them when the trails ice over

## Parking and getting there

Rock House is on State Route 374, the road that links most of the park's trail areas. From Old Man's Cave, it's about 15 minutes by car.

Parking is free, in the Rock House lot at the trailhead. Like every trail area in the park, it has a picnic area with tables, grills and restrooms, and there's a day-use shelter house you can reserve through [Reserve Ohio](https://reserveohio.com). Fall weekends get busy, so come early.

## A short history of Rock House

People were using Rock House long before the trail existed. The state's history of the park notes "hominy holes" worn into the rock for preparing food and troughs carved to collect rainwater, left by the people who lived here before settlers arrived.

In 1835, Colonel F. F. Remple opened the Rock House Hotel at the site. State geologists describe it as a 16-room hotel with a ballroom and a livery stable, and at one point it held a U.S. post office. It ran until 1925. The cave itself picked up a nickname along the way: "Robbers Roost," after the outlaws said to have hidden here.

In the 1930s, young men from the Civilian Conservation Corps built many of the park's trails and bridges. The park credits Company 526, a camp of Black workers who lived on top of Cantwell Cliffs, with the work at Rock House.

## When to go

- **Spring:** wildflowers on the forest floor and fresh green on the hillsides
- **Summer:** the shaded cave is a cool break on a hot afternoon
- **Fall:** color on the hillsides and the busiest weekends of the year
- **Winter:** quiet trails and ice on the cliff face. Bring ice cleats

Any season, a weekday morning is the quietest time to have the cave to yourself.

## Make a morning of it

Rock House is a short stop, so pair it with another trail nearby. Cantwell Cliffs has a 2-mile trail the park rates difficult. Conkle's Hollow, a state nature preserve, has a partly paved gorge trail of about 1 mile round trip.
