In cabin rentals with a hot tub, check 3 things before the photos: the hot tub is private, it seats most of your group and it runs all year. Every one of our Hocking Hills cabins has a private hot tub, open in every season, for groups of 6 to 29. After that, check the pool dates.

## What to look for in cabin rentals with a hot tub

Before you book, ask about:

- Private or shared. If you're comparing lodges with hot tubs, ask whether the hot tub belongs to your room or is shared with other guests. At a private cabin, the hot tub is your group's alone
- Seats for your group. With a hot tub for 6, a group of 16 takes turns. Look for the seat count as well as the photo
- Open all year. Ask whether the hot tub runs in winter, especially for a January weekend
- How far from the door. A few steps from the back door matters at 20°F. A hot tub under a roof is even better in rain or snow
- The pool dates. If you want a swim too, ask when the pool is open
- House rules. Ask about glassware, quiet hours and anything else that applies outside

## Hot tub safety basics

Keep the water at 104°F or below, says the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in its [hot tub guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/safety/what-you-can-do-to-stay-healthy-in-hot-tubs.html). The CDC also says:

- Children under 5 shouldn't use a hot tub
- Don't drink alcohol before or during a soak
- Shower before you get in
- If you're pregnant, talk to a healthcare provider first

In 1979, the US [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov/content/cpsc-warns-of-hot-tub-temperatures) warned that hot tub water should never go above 104°F. It calls 100°F safe for a healthy adult.

Our house rules also ban glassware near the pool or hot tub, so bring cans or plastic cups outside.

## What to pack for the hot tub

Every cabin has bath towels and pool towels, so pack for the walk out:

- A swimsuit for everyone, and a spare so nobody pulls on a wet one
- Sandals or slides for the walk out, and a robe or a warm layer in winter
- Water bottles or cans, since glass stays inside
- A small waterproof bag if a phone is coming out

## Times to plan a soak

Plan a soak for right after a hike. [Hocking Hills State Park](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) closes its trails 30 minutes after sunset, so aim to be back at the cabin and in the hot tub by dark.

On a winter night, the hot tub is where the group warms up. Lazy Deer Retreat, Pickle Hill and Valhalla Hills have a sauna too. Our guide to [cabins for winter](/blog/cabins-for-winter) has more ideas for the cold months.

On a clear night, look up. Clouds Reach has big dark skies and a three-seasons room to watch them from.
