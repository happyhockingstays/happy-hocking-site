How to plan a group trip comes down to a few decisions made in the right order: who's in charge, what it costs, when it happens and where everyone sleeps. Settle those 4, and the rest is details. Skip them, and the group chat runs for months.

We host big groups every week in the Hocking Hills, from 8 friends to 29 cousins. Here's the playbook we wish every group had before they booked.

## How to plan a group trip in 9 steps

This order works for a long weekend or a full week, for 6 people or 25.

### 1. Name one planner

Group trip planning by committee is how plans stall. Pick one person to lead, and give them 1 or 2 helpers: one for meals, one for activities. The planner books the house and keeps the money straight. Everyone else agrees to answer polls by the deadline.

### 2. Agree on the trip before the place

Before anyone sends a single listing, agree on 3 things: the kind of trip (outdoors, relaxing or a mix), the budget per person and the rough headcount. A 2-minute poll saves a week of debate. If half the group wants hiking and half wants a hot tub and a book, good news: a cabin does both.

### 3. Pick the dates with a deadline

Offer 2 or 3 date options and set a deadline to vote. The dates with the most "yes" votes win, even if 1 person can't make it. Waiting for a perfect weekend is how groups end up with no weekend.

Book early. Big houses go first, and summer, fall and holiday weekends fill months ahead. Most of our cabins need 2 nights at least, and holidays can need 3.

### 4. Count beds, not guests

This is where most plans go wrong. A house that "sleeps 14" might mean 8 beds, a pullout and 2 bunks. Before you book, check the bedroom list and match it to your people: couples who want a door that closes, kids who'll take bunks, the snorer who needs a room at the end of the hall.

Also check the kitchen and the gathering space. A big group needs a table everyone fits around and a kitchen that 3 cooks can share. [Loch Llyn](/cabins/loch-llyn) and [Valhalla Hills](/cabins/valhalla-hills) each sleep 29 and have 2 full kitchens. [The Farm at Nickel Plate](/cabins/the-farm-at-nickel-plate) sleeps 22 and has its own sports barn. Our page of [cabin rentals for large groups](/hocking-hills-large-group-cabins) lists every cabin that sleeps 10 or more.

### 5. Collect money early

Decide how you'll split the cost before you book, then collect deposits right away. Most groups split by bedroom. Once people have paid, they show up. And the planner isn't covering anyone's share for months.

Read the cancellation policy together, too. At our cabins, it's a full refund up to 60 days before check-in. At Valhalla Hills and Loch Llyn it's 90 days, which gives a big group time to firm up plans.

### 6. Assign rooms before you arrive

Nothing starts a trip worse than 12 people racing for the best bedroom. Make the room chart in advance, post it in the shared doc and stick to it. Give the best room to whoever did the most planning.

### 7. Plan meals by household

Give each household or couple 1 meal to cover, from shopping to dishes. Keep breakfasts simple and make 1 dinner the big one. Do the main grocery run on the way in. If you're coming to the Hocking Hills, see our guide to [grocery stores in Logan, Ohio](/blog/grocery-stores-in-logan-ohio).

### 8. Plan 1 shared thing a day

The best group trips have 1 thing everyone does together each day and a lot of free time around it. A morning hike, a game tournament, a long dinner. Don't schedule every hour: people want time to nap, read or sit by the pool, which is open May 15 to October 15 at most of our cabins.

For a Hocking Hills trip, the paved quarter-mile trail to [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave) works for every age. [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave) is a 1-mile trail for the ones with more energy.

### 9. Write one trip sheet

The group chat is for jokes. Decisions go in one shared doc: the address, check-in and check-out times, the room chart, the meal schedule, the house rules and a packing list. Send it about 2 weeks before the trip, then once more the day before.

## Group trip planning: the timeline

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 3 to 6 months out | Name the planner, poll for budget and dates, book the house |
| 2 to 3 months out | Collect deposits, make the room chart, pick the 1 shared thing a day |
| 1 month out | Assign meals, collect the rest of the money |
| 2 weeks out | Send the trip sheet with the house rules and packing list |
| Day before | Resend the address and arrival time |

## How to plan a group vacation everyone enjoys

The logistics are the easy part. The harder part is a trip that works for the early risers and the night owls, the hikers and the loungers.

- **Pick a house with spaces to split up.** A game room, a porch or a second living room lets 4 people play cards while 6 watch a movie
- **Respect the quiet people.** Not everyone wants 3 days of constant group time. Build in a slow morning
- **Plan for the youngest and oldest.** Short trails, a pool with shallow steps and a bedroom on the main floor matter more than you'd think
- **Have a rainy-day plan.** A board game tournament or a trip into town. Our [family reunion games](/blog/family-reunion-games) guide has ideas that work for any big group
- **End on time.** Check-out at our cabins is 11:00 am, or 10:00 am at Loch Llyn. Start packing the night before

## Know the house rules before you go

Every rental has rules, and big groups are the ones most likely to bump into them. Share them in the trip sheet so nobody's surprised.

At our cabins, every guest counts toward the cabin's maximum. Parties and events aren't allowed. Visitors need host approval, so ask us before you invite anyone who isn't staying. There's no smoking and no pets, and each cabin lists its own quiet hours. We prefer the booking guest to be 25 or older.

None of that gets in the way of a good trip. It's what keeps the house in shape for the next group, and the neighbors happy.
