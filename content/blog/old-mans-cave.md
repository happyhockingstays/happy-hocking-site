Old Man's Cave is a half-mile sandstone gorge in Hocking Hills State Park, south of Logan, Ohio, with 2 main waterfalls, a huge recess cave and a 1-mile trail that takes about an hour. It's free, it's open every day of the year and it's where the park's visitor center is. For most people, Old Man's Cave, Hocking Hills State Park's best-known trail, is the first stop of the trip.

It's the first place we point our guests to, with the same advice every time: go early, wear real shoes and don't stop at the first waterfall. Here's the longer version.

## The basics at a glance

- **Where:** State Route 664, about 25 minutes south of downtown Logan
- **Trail:** 1 mile, rated moderate by the park
- **Hours:** a half hour before sunrise to a half hour after sunset, all year
- **Cost:** free, parking included
- **Visitor center:** open daily, 10:00 am to 4:00 pm (hours can change with the season)
- **Good to know:** stone steps, bridges and tunnels, so no strollers or wheelchairs on the gorge trail

Those details come from the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, which runs the park. Check the [Hocking Hills State Park page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) before you go, because trails close now and then for repairs or weather.

## Where to park at Old Man's Cave

Park at the visitor center, right off State Route 664. It's the main lot for this part of the park, and the trail starts a short walk from your car.

Summer and fall weekends are the busy times. Go first thing in the morning or late in the afternoon and the gorge is much quieter. Weekdays are quieter still.

Stop inside the Old Man's Cave visitor center before you hike. The upper level has trail maps and safety displays, the lower level covers the history and geology of the gorge, and there are restrooms and places to fill a water bottle.

## The Old Man's Cave trail, stop by stop

Old Man's Creek carved this gorge through about 150 feet of Blackhand sandstone. The park describes it in 5 parts: Upper Falls, the Upper Gorge, Middle Falls, Lower Falls and the Lower Gorge. The trails here are signed one way, so follow the arrows and you'll see them in order.

### Upper Falls

The trail begins at Old Man's Cave Upper Falls, where the creek drops into a clear pool. It's the postcard shot, and it's the first thing you see. Don't turn around here. The rest of the gorge is still ahead.

### Devil's Bathtub

A short way downstream, the creek spins through a round basin it has worn into the sandstone. The swirl is strongest after a good rain.

### The A-frame bridge and the cave

Past the bathtub the gorge deepens and the hemlocks close in. You'll pass the A-frame bridge, named for its supports. Then the cave opens up beside the trail. It's a recess cave: a wide, deep overhang, not a tunnel into the hill. Stand at the back and look out through the trees.

### Sphinx Head and Lower Falls

Below the cave, watch the cliff for Sphinx Head, a rock profile that looks like its name. Then comes Lower Falls, which drops into a broad, shallow pool. From here the trail climbs out of the gorge and loops back toward the visitor center, or you can keep walking.

### Broken Rock Falls

If you have extra time, the Broken Rock Trail leads to a tiered waterfall that's easy to miss. After rain, it's worth the steps.

## Who was the old man?

His name was Richard Rowe. According to the park's history, his family came to the Ohio River Valley from Tennessee around 1796. Rowe later lived as a hermit in the cave that now carries his nickname. The story goes that he's buried beneath its ledge.

He wasn't the first to shelter here. 2 brothers, Nathaniel and Pat Rayon, arrived in 1795 and built a cabin just north of the cave.

## Longer hikes from the same trailhead

The visitor center is the starting point for the park's longer routes, too.

- **Grandma Gatewood Trail.** This 6-mile trail starts at Upper Falls and runs to Cedar Falls and then to [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave). It's named for Emma "Grandma" Gatewood, who in 1955 became the first woman to hike the entire Appalachian Trail. It's also part of Ohio's Buckeye Trail.
- **Whispering Cave Trail.** A 4.5-mile route the park rates difficult. It crosses the swinging Hemlock Bridge and reaches the second largest cave in the region, with a 105-foot seasonal waterfall.
- **The Winter Hike.** The park's 61st [Winter Hike](https://ohiodnr.gov/home/news-and-events/all-events/parks-wc-events/SE-Ohio/winterhike-hocking-11627) is Saturday, January 16, 2027. Hikers set off from Upper Falls any time from 9:00 to 11:00 am and walk 6 miles to Ash Cave, with bean soup and cornbread at Cedar Falls on the way. A shuttle takes you back to your car.

## Where to take Old Man's Cave photos

Most of the pictures you've seen online come from 4 spots:

1. **Upper Falls,** from the edge of the pool
2. **Devil's Bathtub,** looking down from the bridge above it
3. **Inside the cave,** facing out, with the gorge framed by the overhang
4. **Lower Falls,** from the trail beside the pool

An overcast day is your friend here. The gorge is deep and shaded, and bright sun leaves hard patches of light on the rock. Waterfalls run strongest in spring and after rain, and they can slow to a trickle in a dry August.

## When to visit

- **Spring:** the waterfalls are at their fullest and the wildflowers are out
- **Summer:** the hemlocks keep the gorge shaded on hot days, and weekends are busy
- **Fall:** the color is the draw in October, and so are the crowds
- **Winter:** the quietest season, with ice on the falls when it's cold enough

In winter, wear boots with real grip and bring traction cleats if there's ice. Our guide to [cabins for winter](/blog/cabins-for-winter) has more on cold-weather trips.

## Trail rules worth knowing

Stay on the marked trails. The cliffs here are high and the rock is slick when wet, so the view from the path is the one to take. Keep kids close near the edges, follow the one-way signs and carry out what you carry in.
