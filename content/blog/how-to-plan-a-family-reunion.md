Wondering how to plan a family reunion without it taking over your life? Planning one is easier when you break it into steps: pick a planner, set a date, agree on a budget, find a place everyone can stay, then build a simple program around meals, activities and downtime. Here's the step-by-step version, from hosts who welcome big family groups to the Hocking Hills.

The Hocking Hills are one of the best places in Ohio to bring everyone together, with big cabins, outdoor fun for every age and plenty of quiet. But the steps below work wherever your family gathers.

## How to plan a family reunion in 8 steps

### 1. Pick a planner and a small team

Every reunion needs one family reunion planner: the person who keeps the list, sends the updates and makes the final calls. Give them 2 or 3 helpers, one each for food, activities and money. A small team decides faster than a group chat of 40.

### 2. Set the date and the headcount

Send a short survey with 2 or 3 date options and ask who's coming, how many adults and kids, and any needs, like a first-floor bedroom or a crib. Summer and holiday weekends are popular, so the earlier you lock the date, the more people can make it. A 2- or 3-night stay gives everyone time to settle in.

### 3. Build the family reunion budget

Add up the big costs first: lodging, food and any outings. Then decide how to split it, by household or by adult, with kids free or at a lower share. Collect a deposit when people commit, so the planner isn't covering everything up front.

### 4. Find the right place to stay

Start with a home that fits your group. Look for multiple bedrooms, a big kitchen, bunk rooms, game rooms and outdoor extras like a hot tub and a fire pit. The right cabin keeps everyone close while still giving people private space.

### 5. Plan the family reunion program

The program is your rough schedule. Keep it light: a welcome on the first night, shared meals, 1 big group activity a day and plenty of free time. Here's a simple 2-night agenda:

- **Friday:** arrive at check-in, 4:00 pm. Pizza night, name tags and a fire pit welcome
- **Saturday morning:** a big breakfast, then a group hike. Ash Cave is short and stroller-friendly
- **Saturday afternoon:** free time. Pool, naps, games or a trip into town
- **Saturday evening:** the main family dinner, a group photo and a tradition, like a talent show or a slideshow of old photos
- **Sunday:** a slow breakfast, a last walk and goodbyes before check-out

### 6. Share meals and traditions

Make a meal plan and split up the cooking so everyone pitches in. Give each household 1 meal to cover, and keep breakfasts simple. Eat together around the fire pit or the big dining table. Think about starting a tradition, like a family talent show or a game night.

### 7. Plan activities for every age

Make a list of things to do that work for everyone. Short hikes like Ash Cave are stroller-friendly, while older kids might love ziplining or canoeing. Plan outings around mealtimes to keep everyone energized. Back at the cabin, a pool, a game room or a court keeps cousins busy for hours. Our list of [things to do in Hocking Hills for adults](/blog/things-to-do-in-hocking-hills-for-adults) has ideas for the grown-ups too.

### 8. Send updates and a what-to-bring list

A month out, send the schedule, the address details (they come with your booking confirmation), who's cooking what and a what-to-bring list. Send a short reminder the week before.

## Planning a family reunion: the timeline

- **9 to 12 months out:** pick the planner, survey the family, set the date and budget, book the place
- **6 months out:** collect deposits and confirm the headcount
- **3 months out:** plan the program and the meals, and book any outings
- **1 month out:** send the schedule and the what-to-bring list
- **The week of:** final headcount, grocery list and a reminder message

## Family reunion planning checklist

- A planner and a small team
- Dates and a headcount, with adults, kids and any special needs
- A budget and a plan for splitting it
- A place to stay with enough beds, baths and kitchen space
- A simple program, with free time built in
- A meal plan, with a household assigned to each meal
- Activities for little kids, teens, adults and grandparents
- A group photo plan
- A what-to-bring list and a reminder the week before

## Leave room to breathe

One of the best things about a big cabin is room to breathe. Give people time to relax on their own or explore the area at their own pace. Building in downtime keeps the mood light.

## Capture the memories

Bring a tripod and take a group photo at a scenic overlook. Set up a shared photo album, or make a photo book after the trip. Those are the moments everyone will look back on.
