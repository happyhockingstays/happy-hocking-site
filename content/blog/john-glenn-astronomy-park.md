John Glenn Astronomy Park (JGAP) is a free stargazing park in Hocking Hills State Park, less than a mile west of Old Man's Cave on State Route 664. From early March through Thanksgiving, volunteers run programs on Friday and Saturday nights around sunset, weather permitting. Bring a blanket and a free parking pass, and the volunteers handle the telescopes.

## How the program nights work

Programs start around sunset on Friday and Saturday nights, and the park's calendar lists the time for each night. Volunteers bring telescopes and share the views, so you don't need your own. Blankets and lawn chairs are welcome, especially on meteor shower nights.

The big telescope is in the observatory, which has a roll-off roof. When the park opened in 2018, [Hocking College](https://blog.hocking.edu/john-glenn-astronomy-park-opening-june-21) described a 28-inch telescope. Viewing through it usually means climbing a short ladder.

Programs depend on the sky. If it's raining or very cloudy, the night is called off. The park posts whether a program is on by noon the same day, on its website and its Facebook page.

### Getting a parking pass

The lot is small, so drivers need a free parking pass on program nights. Passes are posted at registration.jgap.org, and more are released in waves, often by the Wednesday of program week. If your date shows as full, check back then.

The first passes go up several weeks ahead. No pass? Once the lot starts to clear, about 2 hours after the start, parking is first come, first served. You'll likely miss the talk, but you may still get a turn at a telescope.

Don't park along State Route 664. The park's director has called that dangerous and illegal.

## Visiting the astronomy park on your own

On nights without a program, you can drive into the astronomy park in [Hocking Hills State Park](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) without a pass. The plaza is open at all times except during special events. Bring your own telescope or binoculars, or just a chair.

A few rules and details, from the park's FAQ:

- There are no public telescopes to use on your own
- No campfires, grills or open flames
- No camping
- The restrooms are outhouses, with no running water
- Leashed pets are fine during the day, but the park discourages them after dark
- 2 accessible parking spots and a paved, accessible plaza. On program nights, ask a volunteer to lower a telescope's eyepiece

### The observatory and the solar plaza

Beyond the observatory, there's a solar plaza with a human-sized sundial: stand on it and your body tells the time. The park is named for John Glenn, the first American to orbit Earth. You're welcome to set up your own telescope on the plaza too, on program nights or any other night.

## Make a day of it

Here's how we'd do it. Hike [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave) in the afternoon, then drive into Logan for dinner, about 25 minutes away. Get back before sunset to park and set up.

What to bring on a program night:

- Your parking pass email, to show the parking attendant
- A blanket or a lawn chair
- Binoculars, if you have them
- Warm layers, even in summer, since you'll be standing still after dark
- Snacks and water, because there's no running water on site

## Taking John Glenn Astronomy Park photos

For photos, pick a clear, moonless night. The Friends of the Hocking Hills State Park say those nights show off the dark skies here.

Bring a tripod and arrive before sunset, so you can set up while you can still see the ground. On program nights, astro-imagers often share live views on their computer screens, which is a good way to see what a long exposure picks up.

## Is there a dark sky park in Ohio?

Yes. Observatory Park in Geauga County, in northeast Ohio, was the first place in Ohio designated an International Dark Sky Park, according to [Ohio Magazine](https://www.ohiomagazine.com/travel/article/visit-ohio-s-first-international-dark-sky-park-in-geauga-county). The International Dark-Sky Association granted it silver-tier status in 2011.

For stargazing in Ohio without the drive to Geauga County, try JGAP. It sits inside Hocking Hills State Park, and its programs are free.
