Lake Logan State Park is a 400-acre lake in the hills just west of Logan, Ohio, and it's the calm side of a Hocking Hills trip. Motors top out at 10 horsepower, so the water stays quiet for paddlers. There's a free swimming beach, a marina with pontoons and kayaks and good fishing. After a morning on the trails, it's the afternoon.

## Lake Logan State Park at a glance

- **Where:** Lake Logan Road, just west of Logan
- **The lake:** 400 acres, motors up to 10 horsepower, a 10 mph speed limit
- **The beach:** 527 feet of sand on the north shore, free and open all year, no lifeguards
- **Rentals:** Lake Logan Marina, spring through fall
- **Fishing:** bluegill, crappie, bass, muskie and catfish
- **Picnics:** 6 picnic areas around the lake and a shelter house you can reserve

## The Lake Logan beach

The Lake Logan beach is a 527-foot public swimming beach on the north shore, on Lake Logan Road. It's free, it's open all year and there are restrooms nearby.

There are no lifeguards, so keep an eye on kids and newer swimmers. From Memorial Day to Labor Day, the Ohio Department of Health posts water quality advisories for state park beaches on its BeachGuard site, so it's worth a quick check before a swim. Boaters have their own swim area at the south end of the lake.

## Lake Logan Marina: boats, kayaks and paddleboards

[Lake Logan Marina](https://www.lakeloganmarina.com/faq) is the place to rent a boat. The state park says it rents canoes, kayaks, paddleboards, pedal boats and pontoons in spring, summer and fall.

How it works, from the marina's own FAQ:

- **Pontoons** go out from the marina, with fuel and life jackets included. There's a 2-hour minimum, with 1-hour rentals only in the last hour of the day. 2 hours is enough to tour the lake, and 3 or more is better for fishing or a swim off the boat
- **Kayaks, paddleboards and pedal boats** rent from the Beach House, about a mile from the marina
- **Bring a driver's license.** Everyone renting needs one, and pontoon captains must be 18 or older
- **Arrive 15 minutes early** for the waiver, life jackets and instructions
- **No tubing and no jet skis.** The lake isn't for either
- **Hours:** the marina opens on weekends from mid-April, then daily from 9:00 am to 8:00 pm from Memorial Day weekend through September 30. The Beach House runs daily from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day weekend. Shoulder-season days depend on the weather, so call ahead

Bringing your own kayak? Lake Logan is a state park and open to everyone, so you can launch from the beach. There's also a boat ramp on the southeast side of the lake off Lake Logan Road and a smaller one about 1.5 miles northeast.

## Fishing at Lake Logan

The state park lists good numbers of bluegill, crappie, bass, muskie and catfish. Anyone 16 or older needs an Ohio fishing license.

Fish from shore, from your own boat or from a rented pontoon. The marina allows fishing anywhere on the lake except the swimming areas. The park has a downloadable lake fishing map on its [state park page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/lake-logan-state-park).

## Picnics, trails and a day at the lake

There are 6 picnic areas around Lake Logan with big parking lots. Most have grills and restrooms. Fires go in the grills only. A day-use shelter house on Lake Logan Road, across from the beach, has electricity and a charcoal grill, and you can reserve it through [Reserve Ohio](https://reserveohio.com).

If you'd like a walk, 1.25 miles of Ohio's Buckeye Trail run through the northwest end of the park toward Hocking State Forest.

A few rules worth knowing before you pack the cooler: the marina reminds renters that state law prohibits alcohol on state park beaches and lakes, and it doesn't allow cooking or grilling on its boats.

## A little Lake Logan history

The lake was built in 1955 for recreation, and it was first called Hocking Lake. The state renamed it Lake Logan to honor the area's Native American heritage and to avoid confusion with Hocking Hills State Park.

The town and the lake share the name of James John Logan, a Mingo chief. Thomas Worthington established Logan in 1816, and a Wyandot village called Oldtown once stood on the Hocking River about a mile north of town.
