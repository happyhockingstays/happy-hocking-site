The Hocking County lodging tax is 6% of what a guest pays for a short-term stay: 3% goes to the county and 3% to the township where the home sits. If you rent a cabin here for fewer than 30 days at a time, you register with the county, collect the tax from your guests and file a return every month.

We co-host vacation homes across the Hocking Hills, and most of them are registered in Hocking County. Here's how it works, what's still changing in Ohio and what to check before you list. It's a plain-language guide, not legal or tax advice. For your own home, ask the [county's lodging tax office](https://hocking.oh.gov/node/20) or your accountant.

## How the Hocking County lodging tax works

- **The rate:** 6% in total. 3% goes to Hocking County and 3% goes to the township.
- **What's taxed:** the rent, plus the cleaning fee and any other fees you charge the guest for the stay.
- **What isn't:** a cleaning fee the guest pays straight to a separate cleaning company. If it comes to you as part of the booking, it's taxed.
- **Who's exempt:** almost nobody. Schools, churches and Ohio state and local government employees still pay. Federal employees and employees of other states are the exceptions.
- **Payments in installments:** remit the tax once the guest has paid in full or has stayed.

One thing surprises new owners more than anything else. The county says booking sites, Airbnb and Vrbo included, don't remit this tax for you. The owner, or whoever files for the owner, stays responsible.

## Registering your rental

Registration comes first, before your first guest.

1. Fill out the county's Lodging Tax Registration Form and send it to the lodging tax office. The form and the email address are on the county's lodging tax page.
2. You'll get a registration certificate with a number on it. Put that number on your online listings, and use it when a booking site asks for one.
3. Use the business name on your certificate on every form and payment you send.

That number is the "Hocking County ID" you'll see near the bottom of our cabin pages, like the one for [Lazy Deer Retreat](/cabins/lazy-deer-retreat).

Before you register, make sure the home is actually in Hocking County. Mailing addresses don't always follow county lines. [Rickenbacker Ridge](/cabins/rickenbacker-ridge) has a Sugar Grove address, and it's registered in Hocking County. The county auditor's parcel record settles it.

## Filing every month

The return is monthly and covers the month before.

- **Due date:** the last day of the following month, weekends and holidays included. October's return is due by November 30.
- **No bookings?** File anyway. The county accepts a zero return.
- **Online:** since May 1, 2026, the county's portal takes the county share and the township share in one payment, for townships that take part.
- **By mail:** a separate form and check for the county and for each township, each clearly labeled. The county won't accept unlabeled forms or payments.
- **Late:** a 10% penalty, and up to 25% once a payment is more than 30 days late.

### Which townships file through the county

The county collects the township's 3% for Falls, Good Hope, Green, Marion, Perry, Salt Creek, Starr and Ward townships, so one filing covers both. In Benton, Laurel and Washington townships, the township's 3% goes straight to that township. The county portal takes only the county's share there.

## Ohio lodging tax and sales tax

"Ohio lodging tax" usually means 2 different layers.

The first is local. State law lets counties, townships and cities set their own lodging taxes, and that's what the Hocking County lodging tax is. If a home sits in a neighboring county, that county sets its own rate and its own filing rules.

The second is state sales tax. Ohio charges sales tax on lodging furnished by a "hotel," which the state defines as a place with 5 or more rooms for guests. Under the [Ohio Department of Taxation's rule](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-5703-9-64), a cabin or house rented as a whole counts as 1 sleeping accommodation, and 5 or more of them are treated as a hotel.

So the Ohio short-term rental tax question most owners ask, whether state sales tax applies on top of the county's 6%, depends on how many homes you rent. Your accountant can tell you where you land.

## Ohio short-term rental laws: settled and pending

Here's what stands today. Ohio has no statewide short-term rental license. Counties, cities and villages set their own rules, which is why the rules can change at a town line.

A few bills at the Statehouse would change that:

- **House Bill 109 and Senate Bill 104** would stop local governments from banning short-term rentals and have booking platforms collect local lodging taxes.
- **House Bill 161** would extend state and local sales taxes to short-term rental stays.

[House Bill 109](https://ohiohouse.gov/legislation/136/hb109) had hearings in the House Development Committee in 2026 and was still in committee when we checked in November 2026. Keep an eye on all 3: any of them would change Ohio short-term rental laws for every owner here.

## Hocking County's draft short-term rental rules

The county has also been working on its own short-term rental ordinance for the areas outside city limits. County planners presented it to the commissioners on February 19, 2026. The [Regional Planning page](https://hocking.oh.gov/index.php/regionalplanning) still lists it as a draft as of November 2026.

The drafts so far have included:

- A county permit for each short-term rental
- Driveway standards, so fire trucks and ambulances can reach the home
- An occupancy limit tied to the size of the bedrooms
- Safety standards for every bedroom
- A local contact who can get to the home quickly when something goes wrong

None of that is law yet, and the details have moved from draft to draft. [WOUB's reporting](https://woub.org/2025/11/21/hocking-county-short-term-rental-businesses-more-regulations/) covers the debate behind it.

Inside the city of Logan, the rules are already in place. Logan adopted its own short-term rental permit in 2023, so a home inside city limits checks with the city as well as the county.
