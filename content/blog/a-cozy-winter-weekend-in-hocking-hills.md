Here's a little secret we wish more people knew: winter might be the most magical season in the Hocking Hills. When the hills go quiet and the snow settles over the trees, the whole region feels a world away, hushed and soft and beautiful. Step outside your cabin on a winter morning with coffee in hand, and that feeling of being one with nature runs even deeper in the stillness. We're a small family-owned business, and winter is the season we most love letting guests in on. Here's how to do it right.

## The winter magic most visitors miss

A lot of people assume you shouldn't visit in winter: if you can't hike, or a few things are closed, why come? We understand the instinct, and we promise it's wrong.

So much of the magic is right at your cabin. Wake up to a snow-dusted view and watch the flakes fall from a warm hot tub. Let the day be exactly as slow as you want it. The Hocking Hills under snow are achingly beautiful, and you get them with almost none of the crowds. Winter here isn't a lesser season. It's a quieter, cozier, more intimate one.

If your group loves to hike, you still can. Plenty of our guests do. They bring the right gear, check the forecast and know their comfort level, because the trails can be slick when it's cold. Do that, and a winter hike to a frozen waterfall is something you'll never forget.

## Get cozy: saunas, hot tubs and snow

The heart of a winter weekend is the cabin itself, so choose one built to get warm and stay warm. There's a special kind of joy in easing into a sauna after a cold day, or slipping into a steaming hot tub while the snow comes down.

- [Valhalla Hills](/cabins/valhalla-hills) has a sauna, a hot tub and a peaceful calm that's made for a wellness-minded winter weekend
- [Pickle Hill](/cabins/pickle-hill) has an infrared sauna for a smaller group
- [Lazy Deer Retreat](/cabins/lazy-deer-retreat) has a private sauna in the primary suite, 5 minutes from Old Man's Cave

Every one of our cabins has a hot tub, so wherever you land, you can get cozy.

## Winter is holiday-gathering season

Winter is when families come together, and there's nothing better than one home big enough to hold everyone, with room to gather and space to slip away when you need it.

For the whole family or a holiday reunion, [Loch Llyn](/cabins/loch-llyn) sleeps 29 and [The Farm at Nickel Plate](/cabins/the-farm-at-nickel-plate) sleeps 22, with big kitchens, game rooms and gathering spaces made for making memories. For a smaller holiday, [Dunkle Creek](/cabins/dunkle-creek) and [Rickenbacker Ridge](/cabins/rickenbacker-ridge) are pure cozy. See them all on our [cabins page](/cabins).

Holiday and New Year weekends are among the first to book, and the large homes go earliest because groups plan far ahead. If a winter gathering is in your future, now is the time to claim it.

## Winter on the trails

A few tips from our guests who hike all winter:

- Wear boots with real grip, and pack traction cleats if there's ice
- Dress in layers and bring gloves and a hat
- Start with an easy trail like Ash Cave, then decide if you want more
- Check trail conditions on [Explore Hocking Hills](https://www.explorehockinghills.com/) before you go
- Head back before dark. Winter days are short, and the hot tub will be waiting
