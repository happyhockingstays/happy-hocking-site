Cedar Falls in Hocking Hills is the biggest waterfall in the park by volume, a wide curtain of water about 50 feet tall at the end of a deep, hemlock-shaded gorge. A short trail with stone steps takes you down to it from a lot on State Route 374. It's also the middle stop on the trail between Old Man's Cave and Ash Cave.

Don't skip it, especially after a rain. Here's how to see it.

## Cedar Falls, Hocking Hills State Park, at a glance

- **Where:** State Route 374, a short drive from Old Man's Cave and Ash Cave
- **Trail:** a one-way loop with stone steps. The park lists the area at 1 mile and rates it moderate
- **Hours:** a half hour before sunrise to a half hour after sunset, every day
- **Cost:** free, parking included
- **At the lot:** picnic tables, grills, restrooms and a shelter house you can reserve
- **Best time:** spring, or any day after a good rain

## The Cedar Falls trailhead and parking

The Cedar Falls trailhead is right at the Cedar Falls parking lot, on State Route 374. That road links most of the park's trail areas, so if you're coming from [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave), follow the brown park signs and you'll be there in minutes.

Cedar Falls trailhead parking is free, and the picnic area beside it has tables, grills and restrooms. There's also a day-use shelter house you can reserve through the state's [Reserve Ohio](https://reserveohio.com) site, handy for a family lunch. On fall weekends the lot fills, so come early or come on a weekday.

## The Cedar Falls trail

The Cedar Falls trail drops on stone steps into the gorge, follows it to the base of the falls and climbs back to the lot. It's signed one way, so follow the arrows rather than doubling back.

How long is it? It depends which park source you read. The park's [2024 trail map](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/ohiodnr.gov/documents/parks/parkmaps/hockinghillstrailmapomctoashcave.pdf) shows the Cedar Falls Trail at 0.4 mile. The [park's website](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) lists the Cedar Falls area at 1 mile and rates it moderate. Either way, it's a short walk with some climbing on the way out. Plan on 30 to 45 minutes.

A few tips:

- **Wear shoes with grip.** The steps and rocks stay damp in the shaded gorge
- **Stay on the marked trail.** The park requires it, and the rocks by the falls are slick
- **Bring ice cleats in winter.** The park recommends them when the trails ice over
- **Watch kids on the steps.** Hold small hands on the way down

## The falls

State geologists describe the waterfall as 50 feet or more in height, and the park's sign at the falls calls it the most abundant waterfall in the entire park. There's history at the top, too. In the 1830s, a grist mill stood at the rim of the falls, using the water to grind grain. Behind the falls, the water has carved a small recess into the cliff.

And the name? Early settlers took the tall evergreens in the gorge for cedars. They're hemlocks, according to the local tourism association, but nobody renamed the falls. The creek that carved the gorge below has its own odd name: it's been called Queer Creek for more than 150 years.

## Hike to Cedar Falls from Old Man's Cave or Ash Cave

You don't have to drive. The Grandma Gatewood Trail runs 6 miles from Upper Falls at Old Man's Cave to Cedar Falls and on to [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave). The park rates it difficult, and it's part of the Buckeye Trail.

- **From Old Man's Cave:** about 3 miles through the gorge to Cedar Falls
- **From Ash Cave:** about 2.3 miles, starting from the top of the steps beside the cave

The trail is one way, so plan a ride back or leave a car at each end.

This is also the route of the park's [Winter Hike](https://ohiodnr.gov/home/news-and-events/all-events/parks-wc-events/SE-Ohio/winterhike-hocking-11627). This season it's Saturday, January 16, 2027. Cedar Falls is the halfway point, where the local Kiwanis Club serves bean soup and cornbread, and a shuttle takes hikers back to their cars from Ash Cave.

## When to go, season by season

- **Spring:** the falls run their fullest, and wildflowers come up along the gorge
- **Summer:** the gorge stays shady and cool. After a dry spell, the falls run lower
- **Fall:** the color peaks in October. It's the busiest time, so come early
- **Winter:** the quietest season, with ice on the falls in a cold snap. Bring ice cleats

The best tip for any season: come the day after a good rain.

## Cedar Falls in Hocking Hills with kids or grandparents

Cedar Falls is short, but it isn't flat. The steps down into the gorge and back out are the hard part. Toddlers in a carrier and grandparents who take stairs slowly usually do fine. Strollers and wheelchairs don't.

For a paved, level walk, start with [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave). The Ash Cave Gorge Trail is a quarter mile, rated easy and listed by the park as wheelchair accessible.
