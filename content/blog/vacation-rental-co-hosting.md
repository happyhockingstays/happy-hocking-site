Vacation rental co-hosting means you keep your home, your listing and your payouts, and a local partner runs the day-to-day work for you. A co-host answers guests, manages the calendar, turns the home over between stays and handles repairs. You stay the owner on paper and in practice, and you decide how much to hand off.

We co-host vacation homes in the Hocking Hills. Here's how it works, how it differs from a traditional property manager and how to tell whether it's right for your home.

## How vacation rental co-hosting works

A co-host is a partner on your listing, not a company that takes your listing over. The usual setup looks like this:

1. **You own the home and the listing.** The listing stays in your name, along with its reviews and its history.
2. **You add the co-host.** The booking sites let you add a co-host to your listing and choose what they can see and do.
3. **The co-host runs the stay.** Guest messages, check-in details, cleaning, restocking and repairs all go through them.
4. **You get paid directly.** Booking payouts come to you. The co-host is paid by invoice or through a payout split you set up.
5. **You talk regularly.** A good co-host sends you a clear picture of bookings, costs and anything that needs a decision.

The point is control. You can see every booking, and if the partnership doesn't work out, you remove the co-host and keep everything you've built.

## What a co-host handles

Every co-host agreement is a little different, so ask for the list in writing. A full-service co-host usually covers:

- **Guests:** inquiries, booking questions, check-in instructions and messages during the stay
- **Calendar and pricing:** keeping availability current and adjusting rates for seasons, weekends and holidays
- **Cleaning:** a full turnover after every stay, from fresh linens to a reset hot tub. Our guide to [vacation rental cleaning](/blog/vacation-rental-cleaning) shows what that covers
- **Maintenance:** the pool, the hot tub and the repairs that always seem to land on a Saturday
- **The listing:** photos, descriptions and house rules that set clear expectations
- **Reporting:** a regular summary of bookings, payouts and costs

Some owners keep a piece for themselves, like pricing or a favorite local handyman. That's the beauty of co-hosting: it bends to fit you.

## Co-host vs. property manager

Here's the co-host vs. property manager question in plain terms. Both can run your rental. The difference is who holds the listing and the money.

| | Co-host | Traditional property manager |
|---|---|---|
| Listing | Stays in your name | Often under the manager's account |
| Reviews | Stay with your listing | Can stay with the manager |
| Booking payouts | Paid to you | Often collected and held by the manager |
| How they're paid | Invoice or a payout split | Taken out before you're paid |
| If you part ways | You keep the listing | You may have to start over |

Many traditional managers collect the booking money, hold it and send you what's left with a statement. That works for some owners. Others want to see every payout and keep the listing they've built, which is where co-hosting comes in. ## How co-hosting works on the booking sites

The big booking sites build co-hosting right into the listing. On Airbnb, for example, an owner can give a co-host one of 3 levels of access:

- **Full access:** messages with guests, edits the calendar and pricing and manages the listing
- **Calendar and messaging access:** messages with guests and can see the calendar, but not change it
- **Calendar access:** can see the calendar only

According to [Airbnb's help center](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1534), only the listing owner can set up a co-host's payouts, and no co-host can see or change the owner's payout method. It also notes that the owner is responsible for the co-hosts on their listing, so it pays to choose carefully.

## Is co-hosting right for your home?

Co-hosting tends to fit owners who:

- Live an hour or more from the home and can't be there for every stay
- Want to keep the listing, the reviews and the payouts in their own name
- Have a home that's already listed, or are buying one and want it run well from day one
- Want a say in pricing and big decisions without doing the daily work

### What to agree on in writing

- Exactly which jobs the co-host takes on, and which stay with you
- How the co-host is paid and what's billed on top, like supplies or repairs
- How fast they reply to guests, and to you
- Who approves a repair over a set amount
- How much notice either side gives to end the arrangement

It's less of a fit if you want to hand off the home entirely and never think about it, or if you'd rather someone else hold the listing for you. A traditional manager may suit that better.
