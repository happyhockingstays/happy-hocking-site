The best pickleball getaways don't involve a sign-up sheet. Some families and friend groups don't do "sit around." You know the ones. The card game turns into a bracket. The backyard turns into a stadium. Somebody always keeps score, even when nobody agreed to keep score. For a crew like that, a cabin with its own court beats a waiting list every time.

And yet, the moment you step outside the cabin here, something shifts. You're surrounded by nature, and even the busiest group goes quiet for a second just to take it in. That's the magic of the Hocking Hills for an active group: room to move and reason to pause, all in the same weekend. We're a small family-owned business, and matching a busy, playful group to the right home is one of our favorite things to do.

## Why plan pickleball getaways around a private court

A court of your own changes the whole trip. You play when you want, as long as you want, with the people you came with. Nobody's waiting for your court, nobody's timing your game and the tournament can run past dinner if it needs to. When you're done, the pool and the hot tub are steps away instead of a shuttle ride.

It's also the easiest way to bring mixed groups. The players play, and everyone else has a pool, a fire pit and a deck to watch from.

## Pickleball resorts vs a cabin with a court

Pickleball resorts are great at some things: lessons, clinics, lots of courts and a crowd to play with. A cabin is great at others. Here's how we'd think about it:

- **Pick a resort** if you want coaching, round-robins with strangers or a pro shop down the hall
- **Pick a cabin with a court** if you're bringing your own group, want the court to yourselves and care just as much about the pool, the hot tub and dinner around one big table

If your group already loves to play, you probably don't need a clinic. You need a weekend where the game, the food and the downtime all happen in one place.

## How to plan pickleball trips for a group

A few tips for planning the trip:

1. **Count your players and your non-players.** It decides the cabin. A group of 8 to 10 fits Pickle Hill or Lazy Deer. A bigger crew fits The Farm, or 2 cabins
2. **Plan around the weather.** Pickle Hill's barn is covered but not heated or cooled, so in summer play early and late, and save the afternoon for the pool
3. **Make a bracket before you arrive.** Doubles, mixed doubles and a final on Saturday night. Somebody will keep score anyway
4. **Pack for recovery.** The saunas at Pickle Hill and Lazy Deer and every hot tub are there for sore legs
5. **Book early for summer and fall weekends.** Peak weekends fill months ahead

## Beyond the court

The best part of an active trip is that the Hocking Hills meet everyone where they are. Do the hikes first, and choose your trail by your group's comfort level. Ash Cave is an easy, stroller-friendly walk to a giant recess cave, while the Conkle's Hollow rim trail and Cantwell Cliffs give your more adventurous hikers something to earn. [Explore Hocking Hills](https://www.explorehockinghills.com/) has trail details and conditions.

When you want a thrill, [Hocking Hills Canopy Tours](https://www.hockinghillscanopytours.com/) sends you soaring on ziplines through the treetops. On warm days, get out on the water with a canoe or kayak trip, or spend an afternoon at Lake Hope, about 30 minutes east. For more, see our [things to do in Hocking Hills for adults](/blog/things-to-do-in-hocking-hills-for-adults).

Start your mornings with coffee at [Hocking Hills Coffee Emporium](https://hockinghillscoffeeemporium.com/) or the [Oasis Coffee Shop](https://www.hockinghillsoasiscoffeeshop.com/).
