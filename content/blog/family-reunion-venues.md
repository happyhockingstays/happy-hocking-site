Family reunion venues come in 5 main kinds: park shelters, rented halls, restaurants, hotels and big vacation homes. The right one depends on a single question: is your family getting together for an afternoon, or for a weekend? For an afternoon, almost any space with tables will do. For a weekend, you want a place where everyone can sleep, cook and spend time together without getting back in the car.

We host big families at our cabins in the Hocking Hills, so we'll say up front which kind we like. But here's a fair look at all 5 family reunion venues, and what to check before you book any of them.

## 5 kinds of family reunion venues, compared

| Venue | Best for | Sleeps guests | Kitchen | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Park shelter house | A 1-day picnic | No | Grills only | Weather and booking the date in time |
| Community or church hall | A 1-day indoor meal | No | Often | Set hours and cleanup rules |
| Restaurant or banquet room | A single dinner | No | Catered | A set menu and a time limit |
| Hotel or resort | A weekend with lots of travelers | In separate rooms | No | Nowhere private to gather |
| Large cabin or vacation home | A weekend together | Under one roof | A full one | A maximum guest count |

### Park shelter houses

For a simple picnic reunion, a shelter house is the classic choice. Hocking Hills State Park has reservable day-use shelter houses at Old Man's Cave, Cedar Falls, Ash Cave, [Rock House](/blog/hocking-hills-rock-house) and [Cantwell Cliffs](/blog/cantwell-cliffs), and the picnic areas there have tables and grills. The details are on the [Hocking Hills State Park page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park). The trade-offs are weather and the clock: you're outdoors, and the day ends when the park closes.

### Halls and restaurants

A hall or a banquet room gets everyone indoors around tables, which is what a 1-day reunion needs most. You'll work around set hours, and out-of-town relatives still need somewhere to stay.

### Hotels and resorts

Family reunion resorts and hotels handle the beds. What they rarely have is a place that belongs to your family for the weekend. People scatter to their rooms, and the reunion happens in a lobby.

### Large cabins and vacation homes

Family reunion vacation rentals put the lodging, the kitchen and the living room in the same building. Grandparents get a real bedroom, cousins get a bunk room and the conversation at breakfast picks up where it stopped the night before. This is the kind of venue we know best, so the rest of this guide leans on it.

## What to look for in family reunion locations

Whatever kind you choose, good family reunion locations share the same 6 things. Run any of the family reunion venues on your list through them.

1. **A reasonable drive for most people.** Map where everyone lives and look for the middle. The shorter the drive for most households, the more of them come.
2. **A bed for every person staying.** Count beds, not bedrooms. Ask who needs a first-floor room.
3. **A kitchen that can keep up.** Feeding 20 people 3 times a day is the real work of a reunion. Look for more than 1 oven and more than 1 refrigerator.
4. **One space that holds everybody.** For the group photo, the big dinner and the slideshow somebody always makes.
5. **Something for every age.** A pool or a game room for the kids, a porch for the grandparents and a trail for whoever needs a walk.
6. **Clear rules.** Ask about the maximum guest count, quiet hours and whether visitors who aren't staying overnight are allowed.

That last one matters, because family reunion venues handle it differently. Our cabins are for stays, not events: each one has a maximum guest count, and parties and events aren't allowed. A family reunion where everyone is staying at the cabin is exactly what they're for. If more relatives are joining for a day than the cabin sleeps, hold that day's gathering at a park shelter house.

## The best places for family reunions keep everyone together

Ask people what they remember from a reunion, and it's rarely the schedule. It's the late-night card game, the kids in the pool and whoever got up early to make pancakes. The best places for family reunions make those easy, which is why a shared house beats a block of hotel rooms.

It can help the budget, too. Splitting one large cabin across several households keeps the price per person reasonable, and cooking together costs less than eating out for every meal. ## What to do once everyone arrives

A reunion needs 1 shared thing a day and a lot of free time. Here, the shared thing can be a short hike. The paved trail to [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave) is a quarter mile and level, so great-grandma and the toddlers can both make it. [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave) is a 1-mile loop for the relatives with more energy.

Back at the cabin, the pool, the fire pit and the kitchen table do the rest. If you're looking for [family reunion ideas](/blog/family-reunion-ideas) or [family reunion games](/blog/family-reunion-games), start simple: a cornhole bracket, a recipe swap and a group photo before anyone leaves.

## Choosing between family reunion venues

Start with the afternoon-or-weekend question, count your overnight guests and pick the place that keeps them together. For a weekend, that's usually a big house with a big kitchen.
