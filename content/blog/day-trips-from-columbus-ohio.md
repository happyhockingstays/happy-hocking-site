Day trips from Columbus, Ohio don't get much easier than this one. The Hocking Hills are about an hour southeast on US-33, with sandstone gorges, waterfalls and the largest recess cave in Ohio. Leave after breakfast, walk 2 or 3 of the best trails and you're home for dinner.

We host plenty of guests from Columbus, and a lot of them first came down for a single day. These are the plans we'd hand a friend.

## The best day trips from Columbus, Ohio start on US-33

Take US-33 southeast out of Columbus. It runs past Lancaster to Logan, the town at the center of the Hocking Hills. From Logan, the main trailheads are a short drive south. Most Columbus day trips to the hills follow that same road, so you can't really get lost.

A few things to know before you go:

- **It's free.** [Hocking Hills State Park](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/hocking-hills-state-park) costs nothing to enter or to park in, like every Ohio state park.
- **Mind the hours.** Trails open a half hour before sunrise and close a half hour after sunset, every day of the year.
- **Start at the visitor center.** It's at the Old Man's Cave parking lot on State Route 664, with restrooms, exhibits and the park's raptors out back. It's open daily from 10:00 am to 4:00 pm, though hours can change by season.
- **Follow the arrows.** Many of the park's trails run one way, so follow the signs and stay on the marked path.
- **Wear real shoes.** Most trails have stone steps and wet rock. In winter they can be icy, and the park suggests ice cleats.

Here are 3 day trips from Columbus, Ohio, each built around a different part of the hills. Pick one, or mix and match.

## Day trip 1: Old Man's Cave, Cedar Falls and Ash Cave

This is the classic, and the one to do first.

**Morning: [Old Man's Cave](/blog/old-mans-cave).** Park at the visitor center and walk the 1-mile gorge trail past 2 main waterfalls, stone stairways and a huge recess cave. It's the busiest trail in the park, so this is the stop to make early.

**Midday: [Cedar Falls](/blog/cedar-falls-hocking-hills).** A short drive away, a 1-mile trail the park rates moderate drops into a shaded gorge to the falls. Every trailhead parking area has picnic tables and grills, so pack lunch and eat it here.

**Afternoon: [Ash Cave](/blog/ash-cave).** End with the easy one. A paved, level quarter-mile trail leads to a sandstone horseshoe about 700 feet across, with a seasonal waterfall dropping from the rim. It's the one stop everybody in your car can do.

Want to walk between them instead of driving? The Grandma Gatewood Trail links all 3 in 6 miles. The park rates it difficult, and it's one way, so leave a car at Ash Cave or plan a pickup.

## Day trip 2: the quieter side of the hills

If you've done the big 3, or you'd rather skip the busiest lots, head north and west of the main park.

- **[Conkle's Hollow State Nature Preserve](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/conkles-hollow-state-nature-preserve).** The cliffs rise nearly 200 feet, and the gorge is only 100 feet wide in places. The Gorge Trail is partly paved and runs about 1 mile round trip to the falls. The 2-mile rim loop is for steadier legs. Pets aren't allowed in the preserve.
- **[Rock House](/blog/hocking-hills-rock-house).** A 1-mile trail the park rates moderate leads to a cave that formed differently from the rest: water widened cracks in the sandstone instead of hollowing out a recess. In 1835, a hotel stood on top of it.
- **[Rockbridge State Nature Preserve](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/rockbridge-state-nature-preserve).** Save this for the drive home. Its sandstone arch is more than 100 feet long and spans 50 feet across a ravine, and the state calls it the largest natural bridge in Ohio. There are 2.8 miles of trail and no restrooms, so plan ahead.

Clear Creek Metro Park is out this way, too. It's one of Columbus's own Metro Parks, and the second-largest of the 20.

## Day trip 3: Lake Logan and downtown Logan

For an easier day, or a summer one, stay close to town.

[Lake Logan State Park](/blog/lake-logan-state-park) has a 400-acre lake where motors top out at 10 horsepower, so the water stays calm. The marina rents canoes, kayaks, paddleboards, pedal boats and pontoons in spring, summer and fall. The swimming beach on the north shore is free and open all year, with no lifeguards.

Then drive into Logan. The Columbus Washboard Company, which moved here from Grandview in 1999, runs guided factory tours by reservation. Main Street has local shops, a gallery and plenty of places for lunch. Our guide to [things to do in Logan, Ohio](/blog/things-to-do-in-logan-ohio) has the full list.

## Columbus day trips by season

There's no wrong time to go. Each season just changes the plan.

- **Spring:** the waterfalls run their fullest and wildflowers cover the gorge floors. Trails can be muddy, so wear boots
- **Summer:** the hemlock gorges stay cool, and Lake Logan's beach is the afternoon plan
- **Fall:** the color peaks in October, and so do the crowds. Get to the first trailhead early
- **Winter:** the quietest season, with ice on the cliffs when it's cold enough. This season the park's Winter Hike is Saturday, January 16, 2027, with starts from 9:00 to 11:00 am

One more idea: stay late for the stars. The John Glenn Astronomy Park is the only part of the state park that's open at night. It hosts programs on clear Friday and Saturday nights from March to late November, and you need to register for parking to attend.
