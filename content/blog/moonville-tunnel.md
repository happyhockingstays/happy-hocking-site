The Moonville Tunnel is a brick-lined railroad tunnel in Zaleski State Forest, about 10 minutes by car from the [Lake Hope State Park](/blog/lake-hope-state-park) office in Vinton County. It's free to visit and open from 6:00 am to 11:00 pm. You park by the old rail bed and walk in.

The tunnel is one of the few pieces left of Moonville, a coal town that emptied out by 1947. It makes an easy afternoon add-on to a day at Lake Hope or Ash Cave.

## Moonville Tunnel location and directions

The Moonville Tunnel sits on the old rail bed inside Zaleski State Forest, Ohio's second-largest state forest at 27,822 acres. The Ohio Department of Natural Resources (ODNR) gives directions rather than a street address, so save them before you head out.

From US 33 near Nelsonville, [ODNR's directions](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/moonville-tunnel) say to drive south on State Route 278 toward Lake Hope State Park. Past the lake, turn left on Wheelabout Road and stay straight. The road turns to gravel and crosses a one-lane bridge.

Coming from Athens, stay on US 50 where it splits from US 32, turn right on State Route 278 toward Zaleski, then turn right on Wheelabout Road. Either way, you'll park near the old rail bed and cross Raccoon Creek to the trail. From there, the tunnel is about 100 yards on.

### Moonville Tunnel parking

The parking area sits by the old rail bed at the end of ODNR's directions. If your map app can't find it, enter 39.3083, -82.3242, where OpenStreetMap, the free public map, places the parking area.

Hope Schoolhouse, a renovated one-room school on Wheelabout Road across from the Lake Hope dam, is a trailhead for the Moonville Rail Trail. It's about a 10-minute drive from the tunnel parking area, so park by the old rail bed if the tunnel is all you want to see.

## Walking the Moonville Rail Trail

The Moonville Rail Trail follows the old rail bed for about 10 flat miles through Zaleski State Forest, the communities of Zaleski and Mineral and the wetlands near Lake Hope. The Vinton County Park District runs it, and a volunteer nonprofit, the Moonville Rail Trail Association, maintains it.

Hikers, horseback riders and cyclists share it. Water sits on both sides of the trail, so the corridor is a wetland, home to many kinds of plants and animals.

The association has put in 11 bridges and plans to install the last 2, with grants already awarded. The trail also passes a second tunnel. King's Hollow Tunnel, also called King Switch Tunnel, is 120 feet long, cut through rock and lined with 12-by-12 wooden beams.

The trail rules, from [Vinton County's trail page](https://vintoncounty.com/moonville-rail-trail-association/):

- The trail and both tunnels are open 6:00 am to 11:00 pm, every day
- A visit after 11:00 pm, a guided hike or an event needs a special use permit from the Vinton County Park District
- Pets stay on a leash
- No motor vehicles, camping, fires, spray painting or littering

Questions about the trail go to the park district at 740-596-7013.

## What happened to Moonville, Ohio

Moonville was a small coal town that emptied out as its mines closed. The last family left in 1947.

In 1856, a landowner named Samuel Coe let the Marietta and Cincinnati Railroad lay track across his property for free. He wanted an easy way to ship the coal and clay on his land. The railroad got a better route between Marietta and Cincinnati.

Moonville grew up beside the tracks. ODNR says it was mostly miners and a few railroad workers, about 100 people at its peak in the 1800s. The mines began closing in the early 1900s, and the town shrank with them.

Only 3 pieces of the Moonville ghost town remain: the tunnel, the old schoolhouse's foundation and the Moonville cemetery. Vinton County's [visitors bureau](https://visitvintonohio.com/history/moonville-tunnel/) says the tunnel was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2021. The [National Register](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/index.htm) is the federal list of places worth preserving.

## Is the Moonville Tunnel haunted?

Vinton County says the Moonville Tunnel has a reputation for being haunted, and 2 ghost stories go with it. According to ODNR, believers say the ghosts of railroad workers struck by a train in the tunnel still wave their lanterns there.

The county visitors bureau tells a brakeman's story. Around 1859, the legend goes, a sleeping brakeman woke as his train left the depot, stumbled onto the track and was run over. His ghost is said to still stumble along the tracks in the tunnel, lantern in hand, trying to catch the train before it leaves Moonville Station.

The county links that reputation to the tunnel's long history of train accidents. Paranormal investigators are welcome, it says.

## Before you go

- Wear sturdy shoes. The trail runs through a wetland
- In hunting season, ODNR encourages hikers in Zaleski State Forest to wear bright orange
- Leave the beer and wine at the cabin. Zaleski State Forest's rules prohibit drinking alcohol or having it out in public
- Bring a flashlight if you want a good look at the brickwork inside
- Want the history first? Hope Schoolhouse is an interpretive center. The county lists it as open March 23 to the end of October, Saturdays 10:00 am to 4:00 pm and Sundays 10:00 am to 2:00 pm

## Taking Moonville Tunnel photos

To frame the brick portal with woods, back up along the trail until the trees fill the edges of the shot.

Morning and late afternoon light is softer under the canopy. ODNR lists fall color among the forest's natural features, so fall is a good time for it. A small tripod helps for shots inside the tunnel.
