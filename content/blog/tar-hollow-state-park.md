Tar Hollow State Park is a 620-acre park around 15-acre Pine Lake, inside Tar Hollow State Forest near Laurelville. Come for a short hike, a paddle on a lake with no gas motors or a swim at the 500-foot beach, then drive up to the forest's 1934 fire tower.

It's open 6:00 am to 11:00 pm every day. Old Man's Cave and Ash Cave are about 40 minutes east. From The Johnny, our cabin in Laurelville, it's 20 minutes.

## Getting to Tar Hollow

The Tar Hollow park office and Pine Lake are south of Laurelville. The last stretch of the drive is on State Route 327 and Tar Hollow Road.

The Ohio Department of Natural Resources (ODNR) describes twisting park and forest roads through deep ravines on its [park page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/tar-hollow-state-park), so take the curves slow.

How long the drive takes, from OpenStreetMap routing:

| Starting point | By car |
| --- | --- |
| The Johnny, Laurelville | 20 minutes |
| Ash Cave | 40 minutes |
| Old Man's Cave | 40 minutes |
| Downtown Logan | 55 minutes |

The park sits west of the main Hocking Hills trails, toward Chillicothe. If you're driving in from the west, it's an easy first stop. Our guide to [day trips from Cincinnati](/blog/day-trips-from-cincinnati) covers the other stops on the way.

## Hiking trails at Tar Hollow

The park has 3 marked trails, and the Homestead and Logan trails both start by the Pine Lake spillway. The table uses the lengths on ODNR's park page. The park map lists 2 of them shorter: the Homestead Trail at 2.1 miles and the Logan Trail at 20.

| Trail | Blazes | Length | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homestead Trail | Blue | 2.5-mile loop | Family friendly |
| Ross Hollow Trail | Yellow | 3.5-mile loop | Moderate |
| Logan Backpack Trail | Red | 21-mile figure 8 | Difficult |

The Homestead Trail is the one to pick with kids or a mixed group. It's a loop, so you finish where you parked.

The Ross Hollow Trail starts in Ross Hollow. Look for its yellow route on the park map.

The Logan Trail crosses the park and runs on into Tar Hollow State Forest. Boy Scouts opened it in 1928.

Part of the Logan Trail doubles as the [Buckeye Trail](https://www.buckeyetrail.org/), a loop of more than 1,400 miles around Ohio. That shared section carries light blue blazes. For a day hike, do a piece of the Logan Trail as an out-and-back.

### Tar Hollow map and trail app

ODNR's Tar Hollow State Park map shows the trails, the beach, the shelters and the fire tower. Download the [park map](https://ohiodnr.gov/static/documents/parks/parkmaps/tarhollowparkmap.pdf) before you go so it works offline. ODNR also lists the park in Ohio Trails+, a trail app built on OuterSpatial.

## The Brush Ridge Fire Tower

The Brush Ridge Fire Tower stands 73 feet tall on a ridge in Tar Hollow State Forest. ODNR's forest map credits the Civilian Conservation Corps, a New Deal work program, with building it. ODNR's sign at the tower dates it to 1934 and has a timeline of forest fire management in Ohio.

The tower sits at the center of the Logan Trail, so backpackers walk right past it. From the park office, it's about 15 minutes by car, on Tar Hollow Road and then South Ridge Road.

Can you climb it? ODNR's pages don't list it as open for climbing, so plan to enjoy it from the ground. If a climb is the reason for your trip, call the forest manager at (740) 887-6275 first.

## Pine Lake: paddling, fishing and the beach

Pine Lake is a 15-acre lake where only hand-powered boats and electric motors are allowed. A launch ramp sits by the beach. In season, the General Store rents canoes, kayaks and paddle boats.

Anglers catch bluegill, bass and catfish here. You'll need an Ohio fishing license if you're 16 or older.

Swimming is allowed in the beach's designated areas only, and there's no lifeguard. Pets aren't allowed on the beach. From Memorial Day to Labor Day, the Ohio Department of Health posts water quality advisories for the beach on its BeachGuard site.

## Picnics, mini golf and the General Store

The park has 3 picnic areas with tables and grills, plus a reservable shelterhouse for groups of up to 50. The shelterhouse has picnic tables, a grill and vault toilets. Reserve it through the state's [Reserve Ohio](https://reserveohio.com) site or by calling (866) 644-6727.

The General Store sells snacks and runs the boat and bike rentals, an 18-hole miniature golf course (there's a fee) and a game room with air hockey, foosball and ping pong. It's open seasonally. ODNR doesn't post the store's season, so call the park office at (740) 887-4818 before you count on a rental.

The nature center is inside the Flex Education Building.

## Tar Hollow State Forest

Tar Hollow, Ohio's third-largest state forest, wraps around the park with 16,446 acres, according to its [ODNR forest page](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/tar-hollow-state-forest). It has a 22-mile hiking network in its northern half and 33 miles of bridle trails. Its 17 miles of paved and 14 miles of gravel forest roads make a good scenic drive, ODNR says.

- The forest has the same hours as the park, 6:00 am to 11:00 pm
- Public hunting is allowed in the state forest in season, so wear something bright whenever you hike here
- The bridle trails are closed this winter, from November 30, 2026 to March 26, 2027
- The state forest bans drinking alcohol in public or having it on display
- In April and May, ODNR says hundreds of people search the hills for morel mushrooms

## Tar Hollow's name and history

Tar Hollow is named for pine tar. Early settlers took it from the knots and heartwood of the pitch pines on these ridges and used it for balms, animal liniments and wagon grease.

The Hopewell people lived in the area from about 200 BC to 500 AD and left burial mounds. The Shawnee and Mingo later hunted these hills.

In the 1930s, a New Deal program called the Ross-Hocking Land Utilization Project bought the land for conservation. Pine Lake and other recreation facilities were built with help from 2 New Deal agencies, the Works Progress Administration and the National Youth Administration.

The state's Division of Forestry took over in 1939. When ODNR was created in 1949, the state developed Tar Hollow State Park from the earlier forest.

## When to visit and where to take photos

In spring, dogwoods, redbuds and wildflowers color the hillsides, ODNR says. Bloodroot and wild geranium are among the forest's typical wildflowers. Listen for wild turkeys gobbling and ruffed grouse drumming in the hollows.

Summer is beach and paddling season. ODNR calls the fall color spectacular.

For photos, try Pine Lake, where ODNR says painted turtles show up along the shore. The fire tower makes a good shot from below, with the forest behind it.
